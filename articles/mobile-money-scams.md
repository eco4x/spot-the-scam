# Mobile Money Scams in Uganda: How to Spot Them and Protect Your Wallet

Originally published on [Spot the Scam](https://spotthescamguide.blogspot.com/2026/11/mobile-money-scams-in-uganda-how-to.html). For the latest version, visit the original post.

A call claiming your SIM will be blocked or a message promising a prize can feel urgent. That is exactly when to pause. Scammers may impersonate a mobile network, an employer or even someone you know to make you share a code or send money.

Here is how to spot common mobile money scams in Uganda and what to do if you are targeted.

## 1. The urgent SIM or account call

A caller says your line is unregistered or your account will be suspended, then offers to help you fix it. Uganda Police has warned about scammers using this story to guide people through a process that takes their money.

**What to do:** End the call. Do not share your mobile money PIN or one-time password (OTP), and do not enter codes or approve transactions just because the caller tells you to. Contact your provider independently using a number you trust.

## 2. A fake prize or payment request

A message says you have won a prize, but asks you to pay a fee to receive it. Or someone claims to have sent you money by mistake and pressures you to send it back immediately.

**What to do:** Do not rely on a screenshot or a message alone. Check your balance and transaction history through your usual official service. Do not send money under pressure. If someone claims a mistake, contact your provider for guidance instead of making a separate transfer.

## 3. Fake jobs that ask for money

An advert may promise a job or interview but demand a registration or processing payment to a personal mobile money number. A familiar logo is not proof the offer is genuine.

**What to do:** Find the vacancy on the employer’s official website and contact them using details you find independently. The Ministry of Health has warned that fake job adverts circulate and says it charges no fees at any stage of its own recruitment. A request for an upfront payment is a reason to stop and verify.

## 4. Suspicious links and login pages

A text or WhatsApp message might ask you to verify your account using a link. The page may look convincing but be designed to steal your details.

**What to do:** Do not open a suspicious link or enter your PIN, OTP or password into a page reached through an unexpected message. Open the provider’s official app or type its known website yourself. An unexpected QR code deserves the same caution.

## If you have already responded

1. Stop the conversation and contact your mobile money provider immediately. Tell them what happened and ask them to secure the account and advise on any transaction.
2. If your PIN may be exposed, change it through the provider’s official service. Do not share the new PIN or any OTP.
3. Keep messages, phone numbers, screenshots and transaction IDs as evidence. Report the incident to police. If your issue is not resolved with the provider, you can also complain to the Uganda Communications Commission.

Act quickly, but remember that recovery of money is not guaranteed. If your SIM unexpectedly loses service, contact your network from another phone as soon as possible.
